The Mystery of Andrée’s Polar Expedition in Easy German

Journey into the frozen Arctic wilderness in 1897, where Swedish engineer Salomón August Andrée set off in a hydrogen balloon named "The Eagle" to reach the North Pole. Learn how freezing Arctic fog weighed down the balloon, forcing a crash landing after just 65 hours, and follow the three explorers' desperate trek across drifting pack ice until their mysterious fate was discovered 33 years later on White Island.

💡 Key Topics & Highlights

  • Andrée and his two companions attempted to fly to the North Pole in a hydrogen-filled balloon in 1897.
  • Freezing moisture and ice weighed down the balloon, forcing it to crash onto the pack ice after 65 hours.
  • The crew trekked across treacherous shifting ice floes for months trying to reach safety.
  • Their final camp, diaries, and undeveloped camera films were miraculously discovered intact in 1930.
