The Great Fire of London in Easy German

Step into 17th-century London during the scorching late summer of 1666. A small spark in Thomas Farriner's bakery on Pudding Lane ignited a catastrophic firestorm that consumed the medieval city. Explore how wooden timber buildings, pitch, and strong easterly winds fueled a four-day blaze destroying over 13,000 houses and Old St. Paul's Cathedral, paving the way for a modern brick and stone London.

Spotify Apple Podcasts Substack

💡 Key Topics & Highlights

  • The Great Fire began after midnight on September 2, 1666, in a bakery on Pudding Lane.
  • Over four days, the fire destroyed roughly 80% of the city inside the medieval walls.
  • Over 13,000 houses and 87 parish churches, including Old St. Paul's Cathedral, were burned to ashes.
  • The disaster prompted the creation of modern brick building codes, organized fire brigades, and property insurance.
