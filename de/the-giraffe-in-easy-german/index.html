The Giraffe in Easy German

Reach for the treetops with the Giraffe (Giraffe), the tallest animal walking the Earth! With necks over two meters long, their bodies boast miraculous adaptations, including giant hearts pumping blood at double human pressure. Learn how giraffes hum to each other in deep, inaudible infrasound at night, why zebras use them as living watchtowers, and how their long, dark purple tongues protect them from harsh African sun and thorny acacia bushes.

💡 Key Topics & Highlights

  • A giraffe's neck has only seven vertebrae—the exact same number as humans, but each bone can be 25 cm long.
  • To pump blood two meters up to the brain, a giraffe's heart weighs over 11 kilograms with extremely high blood pressure.
  • Recent studies revealed that giraffes hum quietly to each other at night using deep low-frequency sound vibrations.
  • Their 45-centimeter prehensile tongues are blue-black to prevent sunburn while browsing thorny acacia trees.
