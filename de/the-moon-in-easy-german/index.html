The Moon in Easy German

Gaze up into the night sky to explore Earth's celestial companion, the Moon (Mond)! Born 4.5 billion years ago when a Mars-sized planetoid collided with the young Earth, the Moon has shaped our tides and calendar ever since. Discover why tidal locking means we only ever see one side from Earth, revisit the historic space race from Soviet Luna probes to Apollo 11 moonwalkers, and learn why the Moon is slowly drifting away from us by 3.8 cm each year.

Spotify Apple Podcasts Substack

💡 Key Topics & Highlights

  • The Moon likely formed from the debris of a cataclysmic collision between young Earth and a protoplanet named Theia.
  • Because the Moon's rotation matches its orbital period exactly (tidal locking), the same side always faces Earth.
  • Only 12 human beings have walked on lunar soil, starting with Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin during Apollo 11 in 1969.
  • Due to gravitational tidal friction, the Moon is slowly moving away from Earth at a rate of 3.8 centimeters per year.
