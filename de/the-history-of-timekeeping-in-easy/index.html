The History of Timekeeping in Easy German

Look at your wrist and wonder: why do we divide our days into 24 hours and 60 minutes? Trace the evolution of human timekeeping from ancient sky-watchers to atomic precision. Discover how ancient Egyptians used shadow sundials and the stars, how Babylonians gave us the base-60 system, and how mechanical pendulum clocks and modern quartz crystals transformed time into the heartbeat of civilization.

Spotify Apple Podcasts Substack

💡 Key Topics & Highlights

  • Ancient Egyptians divided day and night into 12 hours each based on shadow sticks and star constellations.
  • The 60-minute and 60-second divisions originate from the ancient Babylonian base-60 numbering system.
  • Mechanical clocks with pendulums, invented by Christiaan Huygens in 1656, increased accuracy to seconds per day.
  • Today's global time is synchronized using atomic clocks measuring the vibrations of cesium atoms.
