The Koala in Easy German

Climb high into Australia's eucalyptus canopies with the sleepy Koala (Koala)! While they look like cuddly teddy bears, koalas are actually solitary marsupials with one of the most specialized and bizarre diets on Earth. Discover how they detoxify poisonous eucalyptus leaves, sleep up to 20 hours a day to conserve energy, hug tree trunks to stay cool during Australian heatwaves, and why mother koalas feed their joeys a special substance called "pap."

Spotify Apple Podcasts Substack

💡 Key Topics & Highlights

  • Koalas survive almost entirely on toxic eucalyptus leaves that would be lethal to almost any other mammal.
  • To digest tough, fibrous leaves, a koala's specialized gut fermenter (caecum) can be up to two meters long.
  • Mother koalas produce a special transitional food called 'pap' to inoculate their joey's gut with vital bacteria.
  • On scorching hot days, koalas hug smooth tree trunks, which are several degrees cooler than the surrounding air.
